# "Is this AI?" — Card Deck
### Opening activity, Day 1 · Playful Programming and Artificial Intelligence

36 cards, designed so that sorting generates maximum argument. Roughly a third lean "yes", a third lean "no", and a third are deliberately unstable. Groups sort into three piles: **AI / not AI / it depends**, then defend their sorting to another group.

## Facilitation notes

**The paired cards are the engine of the activity:**

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| 1 vs 2 | Fixed thermostat vs learning thermostat |
| 25 vs 26 | Fixed traffic light vs adaptive traffic light |
| 17 vs 18 | Route calculation vs live rerouting |
| 8 vs 9 | Autocomplete (prediction) vs autocorrect (rule) |

Groups that put one card of a pair in each pile have to articulate exactly what changed. That articulation is usually some version of "it learns from data", which is the concept the activity is smuggling in.

**The biological cards (31–36)** break the "machines only" assumption and set up Day 1's closing discussion about what "sensing", "deciding", and "learning" actually mean. The sunflower and the immune system reliably split rooms.

**The institutional cards (27–30)** seed the critical-literacy thread that Day 2 picks up. These are the cards where "is it AI?" quietly becomes "does it matter whether we call it AI?", which is arguably the more important question for students.

**Closing move:** each group writes their working definition of AI on the wall. The definitions stay up for all five days and are revisited on Days 4 and 5.

**Running time:** 45 minutes » 15 minutes sorting, 15 minutes cross-group defence, 10 minutes whole-room discussion, 5 minutes writing wall definitions.
